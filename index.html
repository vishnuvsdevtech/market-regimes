What you can forecast, and what you can't

Direction is close to noise. Volatility is not — it clusters, it mean-reverts, and it can be modelled honestly. This page shows realised volatility, an EWMA forecast and a regime classification for three assets. It deliberately contains no price target, no signal and no "buy" anywhere.

how to read it

If the medians differ meaningfully and the samples are large, that's a real pattern worth studying. If the medians are within a few percent of each other, the honest reading is "no signal here" — which is the most common truthful answer in this field.

Why this and not a price predictor

Volatility clusters

Large moves follow large moves. This is one of the most robust findings in finance, and it's why every serious desk models risk rather than direction.

Direction resists

If a repeatable edge existed, it would be arbitraged away. Anyone claiming reliable price prediction is selling the model, not the returns.

Regimes are descriptive

"The market is stressed" changes position sizing. "The market will fall" is a guess wearing a lab coat.

limits, stated up front

Daily closes only — no intraday, no costs, no slippage. EWMA is a forecast of variance, not a guarantee. Regime thresholds (0.6 and 0.85 percentile) are conventional, not tuned. Past behaviour in a regime is not a promise about the next occurrence. Nothing here is investment advice.
